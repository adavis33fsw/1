🧭 Field Lab: Venture Expedition

A Desirability, Prototyping, Alpha-Testing & Customer Feedback Simulation

📋 Instructor Quick-Start (expand now, collapse before class)

Objective: Students apply UX desirability concepts (prototyping, product reaction cards), alpha-testing scenario design, and customer-feedback analysis (affinity diagramming, A/B testing, Build-Measure-Learn) by inventing a campus micro-venture, mapping it on a Business Model Canvas, hunting for real-world concept artifacts on campus, and desirability-testing their idea on real people.

Group size: 3-5 students. Each student works individually on the Field and Interview logs even though they share one idea and canvas.

Device compatibility: Works on iPhone/Safari, iPad/Safari, Android phones/Chrome, Android tablets, Mac, and Windows. Photos auto-compress on-device. Every photo field offers both "Take Photo" and "Choose Existing" options. On iOS, the PDF report opens in a new tab — students tap the Share icon and choose "Save to Files."

Data note: Autosaves to this browser's local storage only. Each student should download their Mission Report PDF before closing the tab.

📸 The Field

Artifact Hunt: Individual

Head outside, solo or spread out. Each team member finds, photographs, and explains their own artifacts. For every photo field, you can snap a new picture OR choose one already on your phone/computer — photos are compressed automatically so this works smoothly on any device.

🖥️ Hunt 1 UX in the Wild

Find a real interface or system on campus people interact with: a vending machine, parking kiosk, elevator panel, door, wayfinding sign, campus app screen, or self-checkout. Evaluate it like a UX designer.

    🧪 Hunt 2 Prototype in the Wild

    Find something clearly rough, early, or unfinished: a taped-up flyer draft, a chalk sketch, a beta sign, a mock-up on a bulletin board — low-fidelity, just like a paper prototype.

      ❤️ Hunt 3 Desirability in Action

      Find a spot, object, or sign people clearly enjoy using, not just tolerate. What makes it emotionally appealing — the kind of visual design and microinteraction that makes something desirable, not just functional?

        📬 Hunt 4 Feedback Mechanism

        Find a way campus already collects feedback: a suggestion box, QR code survey, comment board, review sign, rating station — like a focus group prompt or a bug-report form.

          🔁 Hunt 5 Iteration Evidence

          Find proof something on campus was revised over time: a patched sign, updated directory, renovated space, a "version 2" of anything — a physical Build-Measure-Learn loop.

            🎯 Hunt 6 Connects to OUR Venture

            Find an artifact that's a real touchpoint for your specific idea: a competitor, a potential customer environment, or a spot your venture would actually operate in.
